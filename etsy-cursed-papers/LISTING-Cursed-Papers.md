## Description

CURSED PAPERS — Nineteen Prop Handouts for Your Table

The moment a session turns real is the moment you stop describing something and
hand it across the table instead. This is nineteen of those moments: a wanted
poster with your player's name on it, a debt ledger with their name in it, a
letter that was never sent, and a message nobody at the table can read yet.

WHAT YOU GET

Nineteen documents, one to a page, ready to print and hand over.

Part I, The Road — a wanted poster, dead or alive, with a blank left for a name
you fill in yourself. A road sign with the distances to four places, one of
which is wrong. A toll notice from a company that stopped mending the road.

Part II, The Town — a tavern's board of fare, a moneylender's ledger of who owes
what, a quarantine notice nailed to a door, a night watch report written by
somebody who is lying, and a job board with four jobs on it.

Part III, The Church — a proclamation of the rite, an order of service for a
burial, and a prophet's handbill printed at no press and paid for by no one.

Part IV, The Dead — a last will and testament, a letter never sent, and a page
torn out of a diary.

Part V, The Dark — a bargain signed in a hand that is not human, an apothecary's
receipt for things no apothecary sells, a message in cipher, a confession signed
under a name that is not the writer's, and a ransom demand.

ONE OF THEM IS A PUZZLE

The ciphered message is really enciphered. The key word is printed on the sheet,
the method is one your players will work out in about a minute, and the
plaintext is a real instruction that changes what they do next. Hand it over and
say nothing. The solution is at the back if you want it.

THE KEEPER'S NOTES

Nineteen entries at the end of the book, one for each paper: what it plants in
your players' heads, what it is really hiding, and the turn the story takes if
they pull on the thread. Read it once before the session — that is the whole
prep.

DRAWN, NOT PHOTOGRAPHED

Every sheet carries its own damage. The poster has nail holes at the corners and
thumbed edges because it lived on a post. The letter has two folds and a tide
line because it was carried in a coat through weather. The job board is torn
down both sides, the diary page is torn along the bottom, and the ciphered
message is burnt away at one edge. The prophet's handbill is on cheap pulp with
no laid lines, because it says on its own face that it was printed at no press.
None of this is a filter laid over a photograph — foxing, damp, ring stains,
folds, tears and burns are drawn sheet by sheet.

FORMATS

Thirty-three pages. Both US Letter and A4 are included, laid out page for page.
Every paper prints on ordinary paper in an ordinary home printer; it will look
better on cream or ivory stock, but it does not need it. The contents page is
hyperlinked and every part is bookmarked in the PDF outline, so you can find the
one handout you need mid-session instead of scrolling past thirty pages.

SYSTEM NEUTRAL

There is nothing here to convert. No armour class, no saving throws, no levels.
These are documents from a world, not statistics, and they sit beside 5E, OSR,
Shadowdark, Mork Borg, Dragonbane or whatever you already run. They work as
tabletop handouts, as LARP props, and as set dressing for anything that needs a
piece of paper with a history.

ABOUT THE ARTWORK

There are no photographs in this book. Every sheet, every stain and every torn
edge is generated by code written for this project, and every word — all
nineteen documents and the Keeper's notes behind them — was written by a human
for this book. The one exception is the cover plate, which is an AI-generated
image, colour graded and typeset by hand. I would rather tell you that plainly
than have you find out.
